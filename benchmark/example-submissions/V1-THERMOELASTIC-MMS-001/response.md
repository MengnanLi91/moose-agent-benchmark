# Canonical derivation

Let $s_x=\sin(\pi x)$, $c_x=\cos(\pi x)$, $s_y=\sin(\pi y)$, and $c_y=\cos(\pi y)$. The thermal source is

$$
Q=2\pi^2s_xs_y.
$$

The mechanical strains are

$$
\varepsilon_{xx}=\pi c_xs_y,\quad
\varepsilon_{yy}=\pi c_xc_y,\quad
\varepsilon_{xy}=\frac{\pi}{2}s_x(c_y-s_y),\quad
\varepsilon_{zz}=0.
$$

For plane strain with isotropic three-dimensional thermal eigenstrain, the thermal stress coefficient is

$$
\beta=(3\lambda+2\mu)\alpha=0.12.
$$

Thus the in-plane normal stresses equal the corresponding linear-elastic stresses minus $\beta T^*$, while $\sigma_{xy}=3\pi s_x(c_y-s_y)$. The body forces are

$$
b_x=11\pi^2s_xs_y+5\pi^2s_xc_y+0.12\pi c_xs_y,
$$

$$
b_y=11\pi^2s_yc_x-5\pi^2c_xc_y+0.12\pi s_xc_y.
$$

Temperature and $u_x$ are zero on every side. For $u_y$, the values are zero on $y=0,1$, $\sin(\pi y)$ on $x=0$, and $-\sin(\pi y)$ on $x=1$.
